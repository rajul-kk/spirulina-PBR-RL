# Literature check: recurrent cores in RL (LRU/SSM vs LSTM/GRU, burn-in, saturation)

Date of check: 2026-10-12. Sources are primary where reachable (arXiv abstract/HTML, NeurIPS proceedings). Each claim carries a URL. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed from a primary text in this session (e.g. OpenReview blocked the fetcher; some arXiv PDFs were too large or truncated). Page fetches were summarised by a small model, so numbers quoted below should be re-checked against the PDFs before being cited in a report.

Our setting: TD3, recurrent actors, long episodes (2,000+ steps), BPTT through 60-step replay windows, online state reset every N (60 or 600) steps. Observed: LRU 5/5 seeds reach the hardest tier; LSTM/GRU 0-1/5; RTU fails; reset 60 helps early learning but does not close the gap; late-training checkpoints are unstable.

## 1. Linear/diagonal recurrences versus LSTM/GRU in RL

- **LRU (Orvieto et al. 2023).** Linearised, diagonalised recurrence with better parameterisation/initialisation and normalisation; matches deep SSMs on Long Range Arena. The paper makes no RL claims. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349
- **Lu et al. 2023, S5 for in-context RL.** Modifies S5 so the hidden state can be initialised and reset in parallel. Reports S5 beating RNNs on a memory task and on partially observable envs, about 5x faster. In the body: bsuite memory (A2C), POPGym (PPO), meta-RL (Muesli); S5 beats GRU on POPGym "Repeat Previous Hard" but is not uniformly better (GRU equal or higher on noisy stateless Pendulum/CartPole); in meta-RL, setting trajectory length equal to max episode length worked best for both S5 and LSTM. Caveats: few seeds (3-8), no full hyperparameter sweep for meta-RL, LSTM and S5 not parameter-matched in meta-RL. All experiments are on-policy or Muesli; no TD3/SAC. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03982 (details from https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03982v3)
- **POPGym (Morad et al. 2023).** PPO only, 13 memory models, BPTT truncation reported as infinite, max episode 1024 steps, state capped at 256. GRU had the best mean MMER (0.349) versus LSTM 0.255; S4D scored -0.180 and was "unstable and often crashed from exploding weights"; linear-attention models (FWP, FART) were weak; authors conclude supervised-learning rankings are a poor proxy for RL and call GRU the best general-purpose model. This is evidence *against* a generic "SSM beats LSTM/GRU in RL" claim. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01859 (details from https://arxiv.org/html/2303.01859)
- **Memoroids (Morad et al., NeurIPS 2024; earlier title "Revisiting Recurrent RL with Memory Monoids").** Reformulates S5, LRU, FFM, LinAttn as monoids; proposes "tape-based batching" (TBB) instead of segment-based batching (SBB). Uses Double Dueling DQN (off-policy). Claims truncated BPTT degrades recurrent value estimators, zero-start states are a problem, stored states/burn-in "do not fix the underlying gradient truncation issue", longer segments always beat shorter ones in their runs, and for FFM roughly 90% of the Q-value comes from outside the segment. Note: their comparison is among memoroids; the visible text did not include LSTM/GRU results. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09900 , https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09900v3 (read through ~100k of 121k characters; remaining portion not read). NeurIPS page: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/19f7f755908372efb25826d61959cdf9-Abstract.html
- **Fast and Forgetful Memory (Morad et al.).** Named in the task; I did not locate or read its primary page. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Mamba-style models (RLBenchNet, 2025).** PPO only (CleanRL). "Mamba has the worst performance across all environments" in MuJoCo; Mamba-2 comparable to LSTM/GRU at ~5x faster training; on a memory task (Memory-S11) Mamba-2 reaches ~0.96 while LSTM/GRU show no learning beyond random. Authors flag that their Mamba does not reset state at episode boundaries (possible leakage). Mixed picture; on-policy only. https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15040v1
- **Other Mamba-in-RL work** (Decision Mamba https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.19925 offline; Drama https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08893v1 model-based; multi-agent Mamba https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19382v2) exist but I found no off-policy, model-free, long-horizon continuous-control comparison of a diagonal SSM against LSTM/GRU. **Gap in the literature: our TD3 + diagonal-recurrence comparison has no direct precedent that I could find.**

## 2. Ni et al. 2022, recurrent model-free RL as a baseline

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05038 (ICML 2022); details from https://arxiv.org/html/2110.05038v3 (read ~100k of 147k characters).
- GRU vs LSTM: "architecture has a minor effect on final performance"; "no significant difference between LSTM and GRU" (one exception: Walker-Robust worst-case, GRU 931 vs LSTM 766).
- Context length: "large influence on task performance, but optimal length seems to be task-specific"; they tested 5 / 64 / >100 and recommend starting at a medium length (64). Lengths and benefit are mixed.
- Separate actor and critic RNN encoders: shared encoder "increases the gradient norm and hinders learning"; shared failed on two toy POMDPs.
- Off-policy (SAC/TD3-family) is the base; they cite R2D2 for staleness of stored representations. How hidden states are initialised in replay was in an appendix I did not see. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- Caveats: single best variant per benchmark reported, own benchmarks; 17.5x slower than MRPO per step; no evidence about LSTM/GRU failing on 2,000+ step horizons. Their tasks are comparatively short.

## 3. Hidden-state handling in replay (R2D2 and after)

- **R2D2 (Kapturowski et al., ICLR 2019).** Compares zero-start state vs stored state, plus burn-in. Stored state suffers "representational drift"/"recurrent state staleness" from parameter lag, worse with distributed actors; burn-in lets the network recover a better start state before gradients are applied; final agent uses stored state + burn-in. I could not open the primary text (OpenReview blocked: https://openreview.net/forum?id=r1lyTjAqYX); the above is taken from an index page and secondary summaries (https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2019/kapturowski2019iclr-recurrent , https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pingali/CS395TFall25/presentations/cao_recurrentexperiencereplay.pdf). **Primary text [UNVERIFIED]; exact burn-in/trace lengths not confirmed.**
- **Memoroids** (above) argue burn-in/stored-state patches do not remove the gradient-truncation problem; value targets depend on context beyond the segment.
- **Train-window vs inference-horizon mismatch.** Lu et al. report that S5 kept improving beyond the training horizon while GRUs did not (https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03982v3); memoroids report learned recurrent Q-functions "do not generalize well over time" (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09900v3). Both support the concern, though neither is a TD3 continuous-control test.
- **Implication for us:** a 60-step window, zero start (or stale stored state) in replay, and a 600+ step inference horizon is exactly the mismatch these papers warn about. A real linear recurrence with |lambda|<1 has bounded, input-driven memory and a state that forgets its start; an LSTM state started from zero in training but carried for 600 steps at inference visits state regions it was never trained on. This is our reading, not a result from the cited papers.

## 4. LSTM cell-state saturation and state reset

- **Gers, Schmidhuber, Cummins 2000, "Learning to Forget".** For continual input streams not segmented into subsequences with marked ends, the standard LSTM internal state may grow indefinitely and eventually break down; the forget gate lets cells learn to reset. Abstract-level evidence only (full text not read). https://mlanthology.org/neco/2000/gers2000neco-learning , DOI 10.1162/089976600300015015. Note: modern LSTMs already include the forget gate, so unbounded growth is mitigated but not excluded (our project memory records an unfixed saturation bug; that is local evidence, not from the literature).
- **arXiv 1905.13469 "Interval timing in deep RL agents" (Deverett et al.).** Check requested: **it does NOT appear to discuss a fixed reset interval.** The abstract does not; a search of the ar5iv full text found no statement about resetting recurrent state at a fixed interval or at episode start. The only related text is a 100-step BPTT unroll divided into 10-step chunks for policy/baseline losses. Citing it for periodic reset would be wrong. https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13469 , https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.13469
- **Layer-norm LSTM.** Ba et al. state layer normalisation stabilises recurrent hidden-state dynamics (statistics computed per time step). https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06450 . That it specifically prevents *cell-state* saturation is not established by this source; implementations typically normalise gate pre-activations. Treat as a plausible, untested fix.
- **Cell clipping.** Frequently attributed to Sak et al. 2014 (LSTMP speech models), https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.1128 , but the abstract page does not mention it and I did not read the PDF. **Cell clipping as a documented fix [UNVERIFIED].** Gradient-norm clipping is standard but acts on gradients, not on forward cell values.
- **Periodic reset as a technique.** I found no primary RL source that studies a fixed-interval recurrent reset as a method. Our reset-every-N scheme is therefore an engineering choice without published support in what I checked. **[UNVERIFIED / not found]**

## 5. RTU (Elelimy et al., NeurIPS 2024)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01449 (HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2409.01449v2 ; proceedings https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1e616bde0438cb10cb6adf076ae7d336-Abstract.html). Read ~100k of 186k characters.
- Claim: RTUs are a "small modification"/"generalization" of LRUs with "significant performance benefits over LRUs **when trained with RTRL**", and beat GRUs and Transformers on partially observable tasks with far less compute.
- Training method: RTUs are trained with **RTRL**; the LRU/GRU baselines in the equal-compute comparison use **truncated BPTT** with varying truncation lengths (the LRU ablation variants in Sec. 4.1 also used RTRL). Main control experiments use PPO (on-policy); no off-policy (TD3/SAC) results appeared in the portion read.
- Parameterisation nuance: the text read describes RTUs as real-valued with a 2x2 rotation-block form that emulates complex-diagonal multiplication, and also adds a nonlinearity in the recurrence (paper's wording "real-valued hidden units"). If our implementation used an explicit complex diagonal, check it against the paper's real-block version and nonlinearity. **Appendix and later half unread.**
- Implication: the advantage over LRU is claimed under RTRL, not BPTT. Under 60-step BPTT in TD3, RTU failing is not a contradiction of the paper; the paper gives no evidence that RTU should beat LRU under BPTT. Sources do not report RTU in an off-policy setting.

## Does our finding fit or contradict the field?

Partly fits, partly sits in a gap. It fits Lu et al. (S5 beating GRU/LSTM, S5 extrapolating past training horizon), the memoroid arguments (truncated BPTT plus zero/stale starts hurt gating RNN value/policy estimators; linear-memory models with resettable state are better suited), and the RTU paper's own framing (the diagonal-linear family, LRU, is a strong baseline; RTU's edge is tied to RTRL, so its failure under BPTT is not surprising). It sits uneasily with POPGym, where GRU was the best general memory model and S4D was unstable, and with Ni et al., where LSTM and GRU were interchangeable and strong at medium context (64); both are largely on-policy or short-horizon, so neither directly contradicts a 2,000+ step off-policy result, but they warn that "LRU good, LSTM/GRU bad" is not a general law and may be specific to our train-window/inference-horizon mismatch, our reset schedule, or our reward scale. The large 5/5 vs 0-1/5 gap is bigger than any gap I found reported, and I found no off-policy continuous-control precedent, so our result is more likely explained by a mechanism particular to our setup (state-start mismatch, cell saturation, which our own notes flag) than by a general inferiority of LSTM/GRU. Late-training instability is consistent with the representational-drift/staleness and horizon-generalisation issues cited above, but I found no paper that directly reports it for recurrent TD3.

### Experiments the literature suggests (ranked)

1. **Close the train/inference state mismatch for LSTM/GRU.** Replay with stored states plus burn-in (R2D2; e.g. burn-in 20-40 steps before the 60-step loss window), and/or train windows of 600 matching the reset interval (Lu et al. found trajectory length equal to episode length best; memoroids found longer segments always better). Compare against zero-start with identical settings; ablate with the LRU receiving the same treatment. If LSTM gap closes, the cause is state handling, not core class.
2. **Diagnose and treat cell-state saturation directly.** Log |c| and gate saturation versus step-since-reset at inference for LSTM vs training distribution; then test layer-norm LSTM (Ba et al.) and a forward cell-value bound or longer-horizon-trained forget bias. Mark cell clipping as an unvalidated fix (see Section 4).
3. **Controlled core-ablation under identical gradient and data handling.** Following Ni et al. (separate actor/critic recurrent encoders, medium context 64) and POPGym's parameter-matched design: give GRU/LSTM a matched state size and a diagonal-linear control (an LSTM with linear/near-identity cell recurrence, or the LRU with a nonlinear readout) and, for RTU, train with BPTT versus RTRL at least on a short horizon. This isolates "diagonal |lambda|<1 recurrence" from "linear-in-state" and from "trained by RTRL".
